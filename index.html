Champion Starter Kit

Everything you need to know to champion a GSD project, in <5 minutes.

The TLDR: Staying legible matters more than ever.

We should be using Vault proactively to show what's moving, what's blocked, and where support or tradeoffs are needed. Communicating the realities of your project -- what's going well and what's not -- is how we make better tradeoffs, unblock each other, and keep moving. The teams that do this well are the ones that ship well.

Principles

1 Your job as champion

You're the DRI. Not the person who does everything -- the person who makes sure everything gets done and meets Shopify's quality bar. Here's the checklist:

  • Own the outcomes. Being a champion is a big deal. You're accountable for whether this project delivers what it promised.
  • Post weekly updates -- either manually or via the auto-updater in your project Slack channel. Projects go stale after 7 days. Even a one-liner beats silence.
  • Keep timelines & key dates accurate. GA date, phase transitions, end date. Leadership uses these for investment decisions. Wrong dates = bad decisions.
  • Own your status. The hygiene bot will flag projects automatically, but status is yours to own. If there's a real risk, keep it At Risk. Don't flip back to On Track until the risk is actually resolved.
  • Resolve health checks ASAP. They run daily. Failed checks auto-downgrade your status to At Risk or Off Track. You'll get Slack pinged daily until you fix them.
  • Surface blockers fast. When you need support, bring it forward -- either in Slack or by booking a speed review. GSD should surface problems early, not smooth them over. Need a speed review? DM Leah to get one scheduled.
  • Open high-quality reviews at the right time (see Section 6).
  • Champion one project at a time. Focus is the feature.

2 The unwritten rules

These won't be in any official docs. They'll save you pain.

1. Align in your fecta before going to Vault

Your product lead / eng lead / data lead should not be surprised by what shows up in a Vault review. Before requesting a phase transition, stopping, or pausing a project, your project team should be aligned and your fecta consulted. Changes in direction should not come as a surprise -- think of this as another way we default to open.

2. Don't have long back-and-forths in reviews

If a reviewer requests changes and it turns into a thread -- take it to Slack or a call. Resolve the disagreement, then update the review with the final answer. Reviews are for decisions, not debates.

3. Vault is a broadcast channel, not a filing cabinet

Most people use Vault reactively -- to satisfy a review. The teams that move fastest use it proactively to stay legible without extra meetings. Your project page should be understandable by someone with zero context.

4. Rewrite your summary before every review

Forces clarity. Signals momentum. A stale summary from 3 months ago tells reviewers you're not paying attention.

5. Stopping a project is a win, not a failure

It's the successful discovery that something didn't work. Leadership respects champions who stop early over champions who drag a dead project through release.

4 Writing updates that don't suck

Your updates are read by people with no context on your project. Write for them.

❌ Don't write this

"We shipped MVP for Oasis, starting training on Monday."

High-context. Only makes sense if you already know what Oasis is.

✅ Write this

"This week, we launched the first version of our new 'Oasis' pipeline -- a system that automates search model training. Starting Monday, we'll test its ability to improve product search accuracy for merchants."

Low-context. Anyone at Shopify can understand what this means.

❌ Activity-focused

"We had three team meetings to discuss search relevance. The eng team refactored some code, and DS cleaned up training data."

✅ Outcome-focused

"Improved search relevance by refactoring the ranking algorithm -- relevant product clicks up 12% in early testing. These changes move us closer to our Q3 goal of boosting store sales from search."

The formula

  1. What shipped / what changed (outcome, not activity)
  2. What's next (so leadership knows the trajectory)
  3. Blockers / risks (be transparent -- use 🔴🟡🟢)
  4. Links to artifacts (show the work, even WIP)

Prototype checklist (before you move to Build)

  • Technical confidence: architecture / integrations / perf risks validated
  • User validation: workflow tested, key edge cases identified
  • Scoping clarity: Build estimate grounded in prototype learnings
  • Go/No-Go is explicit: build with confidence, re-scope, or stop

6 Reviews -- what to include

Reviews are feedback loops at critical moments. They serve three purposes:

  1. Risk transfer -- you're transferring the risk of investing more resources to leadership
  2. Alignment -- forcing function to ensure we're building the right thing
  3. Feedback -- greater momentum and confidence in direction

Who reviews your project

RoleWhat they do
OK1 (max 3)Leaders with context to judge quality and strategy. Added via team review plan.
OK2 (exactly 1)The final say. Single point of accountability. Usually the aimer.
OK InformFYI only. Non-blocking, but can comment. If they request changes, they become OK1.
SophiaAI reviewer. Provides constructive feedback. Not mandatory to address, but recommended before OK1/2s review.

Reviewer actions

  • Approve -- project moves to next phase (if all agree)
  • Pre-approve -- approved up to a future phase (skips intermediate reviews)
  • Request changes -- you must address and mark complete to get back in their inbox
  • Deny -- project cannot move forward. Review closes.
  • Comment -- question or clarification needed
Pre-approvals are your friend. Some projects don't need a prototype phase. Some reviewers don't need to see every transition. Request pre-approval to skip unnecessary reviews and move faster.

7 Common mistakes

🔴 Letting health checks pile up

Health checks run daily. Every failed check = a daily Slack ping and an auto-downgraded status. Resolve them the day they appear, not next week.

🔴 Dates that haven't been updated in months

If your end date says "March 2026" and it's May, your project looks abandoned. Update dates when reality changes -- leadership uses these for planning.

🔴 Shipping before Release phase

EA, Limited Release, and GA must all happen in Release phase. If you ship in Build, your reviews are meaningless and your releases aren't tracked.

🔴 Proposals with broken links or gated docs

Every link in your proposal must be accessible to all Shopify employees. No "request access" screens. No 404s. Check them before you submit.

🔴 Vague proposals

A proposal needs: clear title (Action + Object + Context), crisp TL;DR, explicit audience, definition of done, estimates, and early success metrics. Leave fields blank = instant "no."

🟡 Using Vault as a filing cabinet

Your project page should tell a story. Tag contributors from other teams (their leads see your work too). Link to your mission. Post milestone updates. Make your work visible.

🟡 Sitting on a struggling project quietly

If timeline slipped >7 days, or project is Off Track with real blockers, or a phase is taking way longer than expected -- book a speed review with SLT. Surface it early.

9 AI tools for champions

Use these tools to write better proposals, updates, and reviews — not from scratch, but with AI help.

🤖 GSD Champion Skill (for Claude, Cursor, River, Mana)

A portable AI skill you drop into any agent. It pulls context from Vault, Slack, and GitHub, generates a first draft, and asks you to validate -- so you're reacting to something, not starting from a blank page.

Install: Run this in your terminal:

devx skills get gsd-champion --global

Works with Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, OpenCode, and Pi.
